# Parameterized families of four-generated semigroups

## Objective and baseline — 2026-09-16

Find explicit parameterized families satisfying `give1683Format`,
`satisfiesDegreeCondition1`, `satisfiesDegreeCondition2`, and
`depthCondition1 >= 2`, covering as much of the genus-at-most-50 sample as
possible. Distinguish proved families from computational conjectures.

The completed enumeration in `experiments/four-generated-genus50/` contains
49,092 four-generated semigroups, of which 27,078 have format (1,6,8,3) and
488 satisfy all three additional conditions. Thus the absolute maximum
coverage using these conditions is 488/49,092 = approximately 0.9941%.
The independent enumeration agreed exactly in every genus.

### Initial approach

1. Audit the two supplied example formulas and their parameter ranges.
2. Inspect additive relations, generator differences, and fixed-multiplicity
   patterns among the 488 positive examples.
3. Derive parameter restrictions from semigroup ideals and graded resolutions,
   rather than use the original tests as circular membership conditions.
4. Measure the union of each proposed family's coverage, including overlaps;
   test new parameter values beyond genus 50 and seek proofs.

### Initial observation

The manuscript and `knownExamples.m2` use `{6,15,g-1,g+2}`, whereas the new
request writes `{6,15,g-1,g+1}`. This will be checked explicitly before using
either as a baseline. The manuscript states `{6,9,g,g+3}` for g >= 13 and
g not divisible by 3, and the second family for g >= 21 divisible by 3.

### Next steps

Create a pattern-analysis script and inspect which short additive relations
and translations account for the largest subsets of the 488 examples.

## First family and structural analysis

- Confirmed the typo by direct Macaulay2 evaluation: `{6,15,20,22}` has
  genus 19 and does not have format (1,6,8,3), whereas `{6,15,20,23}` has
  genus 21 and passes both degree conditions.
- All 126 positive multiplicity-6 examples have the form
  `{6,3p,c,c+3v}`, with p and v odd and 0 < v < p.
- Generalized this to `{2h,ph,c,c+vh}`, allowing the displayed generators
  to be reordered. A proposed six-binomial Groebner basis predicts the
  graded Betti degrees explicitly. Replacing the homological tests by
  arithmetic inequalities on those predicted degrees selects **275** of
  the 488 positives and **zero false positives** among all 49,092 candidates.
  Coverage is approximately 0.5602% of all four-generated semigroups.
- The implementation and exact covered tuples are in `patterns.py` and
  `double-pair-coverage.json` in the experiment directory. This remains
  a proposed infinite family until the symbolic basis/resolution argument
  and tests beyond the original sample have been completed.

Decision: retain this as the first major family, then study the rank-two
relation lattice generated by the first four binomials of each remaining
positive example. The aim is to generalize the common four-generator
subcomplex rather than introduce many isolated fixed-generator families.

## Three constructions reach the target range

The structural extraction confirmed that **all 488** examples have a
rank-two lattice of relations among their first four binomials and that
the ideal generated by those four binomials has resolution (1,4,4,1).
There are five monomial-support types and 72 distinct relation planes in
the sample. This is consistent with the syzygy-quadrangle construction for
codimension-two lattice ideals (Peeva–Sturmfels; also described in Section 2
of Leah Gold's paper, https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0211326).

Three arithmetic constructions have now been implemented:

1. Gap extension: `{hq,hp,c,c+hv}`, starting with T=<q,p>, a gap v with
   2v in T, and c,c+v in T. **337** sample examples.
2. Pinched quadratic: `{a,b,qa+u,pb+u}`, with 2u=Ra+Sb and explicit
   positivity/congruence restrictions. **367** sample examples. Union with
   the first construction: **429**.
3. Scroll construction: `{a,ka+2u,qa+u,pka+(2p+1)u}`, with 0<k<q,
   gcd(a,u)=1, and a congruent to 0 or -1 modulo 2p+1. **36** additional
   sample examples, giving **465** in the direct union.

Each uses explicit lists of predicted graded shifts and integer inequalities,
not the Macaulay2 condition functions, to select parameters. All predictions
were compared with every one of the 49,092 enumerated semigroups:
**zero false positives**. The predicted graded shifts also agree exactly with
Macaulay2 for every covered example and every parameter certificate.

Diagonal monomial substitutions, reduced using the gcd of each triple of
generators, add six more examples: **471/488** positives, or approximately
**0.9594% of all 49,092** four-generated semigroups. The requested numerical
coverage target is therefore reached in the original sample.

Outstanding work before claiming infinite families: complete a written
presentation/resolution proof for the constructions, check that the degree
inequalities select the intended four-generator subcomplex, and test new
parameter values outside the genus-50 sample. The remaining 17 examples
will also be inspected for an inexpensive further generalization.

## Final extension and proof certificates

- Allowing a signed auxiliary u in the scroll construction added ten
  examples. Its coefficient inequalities still ensure genuine polynomials
  and positive numerical generators. Coverage rose to 481 after lifting.
- A fourth family, `{a,a+d,a+3d,a+4d}` with a=4n-1, adds four examples.
  Its degree restrictions simplify completely: gcd(a,d)=1, d>0, and
  either n>=4, or n=3 and d>=6.
- Final coverage: **485/488 = 99.3852% of the positive examples**, or
  **485/49,092 = 0.987941% of all four-generated semigroups**. The direct
  union has 479 examples, and diagonal substitutions add six.

The proof is now documented in
`experiments/four-generated-genus50/FAMILIES.md`. Six generic presentations
were checked over both QQ and ZZ/10007. Each has codimension 3 and minimal
resolution ranks (1,6,8,3); the distinguished core has ranks (1,4,4,1).
The core adjugate identity and the absence of units after all allowed
monomial substitutions were also checked. Cohen–Macaulay specialization,
lattice saturation, and the primitive exponent-lattice calculation identify
the specialized ideal with the numerical-semigroup ideal. Exactness of the
core gives the depth condition, without invoking the manuscript's broader
acyclicity conjecture.

During the generic calculation, an initial use of the standard grading gave
a nonhomogeneous presentation and misleading resolution data. The final
certificates use explicit positive homogeneous weights and assert
homogeneity before every calculation.

### 2026-09-16: genus formula for the quartic family

At the user's request, derived the genus of
`{4n-1, 4n-1+d, 4n-1+3d, 4n-1+4d}`:
`g = 2n(n-1) + (2n-1)d + 2`.
The proof uses the Apéry set modulo `a=4n-1`: the least element in
residue `kd` is `a*ceil(k/4)+kd`, except at `k=2`, where it is `2a+2d`.
Summing gives the formula. Recorded the full proof in `FAMILIES.md`.
Independent shortest-path computations verified both the Apéry set and
genus for all 3,066 allowed pairs with `3 <= n <= 40`, `1 <= d <= 100`.
The four genus-at-most-50 examples have genera 33, 40, 44, and 49.
This resolves the genus question; the previously listed optional research
directions remain unchanged.

### 2026-09-16: comparison with all numerical semigroups

Compared the full successful census (488 examples, including the three
outside the formulas) against OEIS A007323, indexed from genus 0. Retrieved
the authoritative b-file through genus 50 and saved a source-labelled local
snapshot. The user's pasted terms agree with the portion through genus 36.

The successful proportion among all semigroups drops from approximately
5.348e-5 at genus 20 to 3.660e-10 at genus 50. Cumulatively, the denominator
through genus 50 is 262,564,541,019, giving 488 / 262,564,541,019 = 1.859e-9.
This is a different denominator from the earlier approximately 1% claim.

There is a rigorous zero-density conclusion among all semigroups: each
minimal generator is at most 2g+1, by pairing i with a-i for a minimal
generator a, so four-generated counts are bounded by binomial(2g+1,4).
Zhai's theorem gives exponential growth for all semigroups, N(g) ~ K*phi^g.
The same polynomial bound applies to the cumulative four-generated count
through genus G. Both exact-genus and cumulative densities therefore tend
to zero, independently of the specific degree and depth conditions.

Within four-generated semigroups, the successful proportions in genus
blocks 21–30, 31–40, and 41–50 are 0.9460%, 0.9554%, and 1.0394%.
These are compatible with positive conditional density but do not establish
a limiting proportion. No extrapolation or sampling confidence interval is
warranted by this exhaustive finite census.
